Concrete Bags Explained: 40, 60 or 80 lb — Which One for Your Slab?

Walk into any home improvement store and the concrete aisle offers three bag sizes: 40, 60, and 80 pounds. They look similar, but they yield very different amounts of concrete, and picking the wrong one means either running short mid-pour or hauling home far more bags than you need.

Yield Per Bag: The Number That Matters

The difference comes down to one number: yield per bag. An 80-pound bag yields 0.60 cubic feet, a 60-pound bag yields 0.45, and a 40-pound bag yields 0.30. That means it takes 45 bags of 80-pound, 60 bags of 60-pound, or 90 bags of 40-pound to make a single cubic yard.

How Many Bags for Your Slab

For a typical project, the math is straightforward. A 10 by 10 foot shed slab at 4 inches thick needs 1.23 cubic yards before waste, which is 56 bags of 80-pound or 75 bags of 60-pound. Add a 10 percent waste buffer and you are at 62 and 83 bags respectively. A small trash can pad of 4 by 4 feet at 4 inches needs just 9 bags of 80-pound.

When to Stop Buying Bags

A backyard patio slab of 12 by 16 feet at 4 inches jumps to 107 bags of 80-pound — and that is where you should stop buying bags entirely. Any slab larger than 1.5 cubic yards, about 68 bags or 120 square feet at 4 inches, should be poured from a ready-mix truck. Mixing 100 bags by hand takes hours, and the first corner starts setting before you pour the last, creating weak cold joints.

Thickness, Water, and Curing

Thickness is the other decision. Four inches with wire mesh or rebar suits sidewalks, patios, and garden sheds. Five to six inches with heavier rebar is needed for garages, carports, RV pads, and driveways that carry vehicles. Standard cured concrete weighs about 4,000 pounds per cubic yard, so a full yard is a serious load — plan truck access before you order.

Water matters too. An 80-pound bag needs roughly 3 quarts of water, and adding too much weakens the finished slab. Mix to a workable consistency, not a soupy one, and cure the slab by keeping it damp for several days for maximum strength.
