Pea Gravel for Patios and Walkways: How Much Do You Really Need?

Pea gravel is the smooth, rounded stone that makes patios feel like a beach path and walkways look instantly finished. It is also one of the easiest materials to get wrong, because people guess the quantity, order too little, and then pay for a second delivery — or order far too much and stare at a leftover pile for a year. The fix is simple math, and it starts with three numbers: your area, your depth, and the density of the stone.

The Right Depth for Every Project

A standard 3/8-inch washed pea gravel weighs about 2,700 pounds per cubic yard. One cubic yard covers roughly 108 square feet at a 3-inch depth, which is the gold-standard depth for patios, walkways, and dog runs. Go thinner than 2 inches and the black landscape fabric shows through. Go thicker than 3.5 inches and the rounded stones shift underfoot like walking on marbles. For French drains and dry creek beds, 4 to 6 inches is right because the washed stone leaves void space for water to drain around perforated pipe.

The Formula: Area, Depth, Density

Here is the formula professionals use: cubic yards equals length times width times depth in inches divided by 12, all divided by 27. A 15 by 20 foot patio at 3 inches deep needs about 2.78 cubic yards before waste. Add a 10 percent buffer for compaction and spillage, and you are at roughly 3 cubic yards.

Bags vs Bulk: The Breakeven Point

In bags, that is about 150 standard 50-pound bags — which is exactly why bulk delivery wins for anything bigger than a small path. One cubic yard equals 54 bags, and at store prices that runs 250 to 295 dollars per yard, while bulk washed gravel from a landscape yard costs 45 to 65 dollars per yard plus a delivery fee. The breakeven point is just 0.65 cubic yards, about 35 bags. Any project larger than 70 square feet at 3 inches is cheaper in bulk.

Preparing the Base Properly

Before you order, prepare the base properly. Excavate to your depth, lay landscape fabric to stop weeds and keep the stone from sinking into soil, and edge the area with steel, stone, or timber edging so the gravel stays put. For a patio that will hold furniture, compact a crushed-stone base first and keep the pea gravel top layer at 2 to 3 inches. For a dog run, the same depth works, and the smooth stones are gentle on paws.
