Mulch Math: Bags vs Bulk and the Breakeven Point

Every spring, homeowners face the same question at the garden center: bags or bulk? The answer is pure math, and getting it wrong costs real money. A standard 2-cubic-foot bag of mulch looks cheap at under four dollars, but contractors measure in cubic yards, and the two languages hide an expensive gap. One cubic yard holds 27 cubic feet, which means it takes 13.5 bags of 2-cubic-foot mulch to make a single yard. A medium foundation bed of 240 square feet at 3 inches deep needs 2.22 cubic yards — that is 30 bags. A full front yard of 500 square feet at the same depth needs 4.63 yards, or 63 bags. Suddenly the trunk is full, the arms are tired, and the receipt is bigger than expected.

Bags vs Bulk: The Real Math

The breakeven point is clear: once your project passes about 4.5 cubic yards, roughly 60 bags, bulk delivery from a local landscape yard is cheaper even after the delivery fee. Below that, bags win on convenience, especially for small refresh jobs. A small front flower bed of 100 square feet at 2 inches needs just 9 bags, and hauling those home is easy. The mistake is treating every project like a small one and making three trips when one bulk order would have cost less.

Depth Matters as Much as Volume

Depth matters as much as volume. Two inches is right for an annual refresh over existing mulch. Three inches is the standard for weed suppression and moisture retention in fresh beds. Four inches works for deep weed control but should never be piled against tree trunks.

The Volcano Mulching Mistake

That volcano mulching you see everywhere — mulch heaped against bark like a volcano — traps moisture, rots bark, and slowly kills the tree. Keep mulch 3 inches deep and pull it 3 inches away from the trunk so air keeps moving.

Mulch type also changes the math slightly. Shredded hardwood weighs roughly 600 to 800 pounds per cubic yard, and cedar or pine bark nuggets last 18 to 24 months while fine hardwood breaks down within a year. Whatever type you choose, the volume formula is the same: square feet times depth in inches divided by 12, divided by 27, gives cubic yards. Add 10 percent for settling and uneven ground.
