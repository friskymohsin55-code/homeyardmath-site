Topsoil for Lawns and Garden Beds: Depth, Volume and Delivery

Topsoil is the most misunderstood material in landscaping. People order it by the truckload without knowing how much they need, or buy bags one by one for a job that clearly needs bulk. The result is either a half-finished lawn or a mountain of dirt blocking the driveway. The fix is knowing three things: how deep your project needs, how much area it covers, and how topsoil behaves differently from fill dirt and compost.

Topsoil vs Fill Dirt vs Compost

Screened topsoil is natural mineral soil with rocks, roots, and clods removed. It is the only one of the three meant as a growing layer. Fill dirt is cheap subsoil with almost no organic matter — it belongs underneath, raising grade, never as the top layer. Compost is decomposed organic matter that feeds soil life but keeps shrinking as it breaks down, so a bed built from compost alone sinks within a season. For a new lawn or bed, the honest blend is about 70 percent topsoil to 30 percent compost: topsoil gives structure, compost gives life.

Depth Decides Whether It Works

Depth decides whether the job works. Lawn repairs take 1 to 2 inches per pass so existing grass can grow through. New beds take 6 to 12 inches of growing layer over loosened subsoil. A garden bed of 10 by 20 feet at 6 inches needs 4.07 cubic yards. A lawn leveling job of 20 by 30 feet at 3 inches needs 6.11 yards. Whatever the depth, grade must fall away from the house — about 6 inches of drop over the first 10 feet — and finished soil should sit 4 to 6 inches below the siding. Soil piled against siding invites rot and pests, and soil against a tree trunk suffocates roots.

The Weight Trap and Delivery Math

Weight is the trap. Dry screened topsoil weighs about 2,100 pounds per cubic yard, but after rain it can hit 2,700. A bag of 0.75 cubic feet covers only a fraction of a yard — 36 bags make a yard before waste, 40 with a normal buffer. Anything past about 3 cubic yards is almost always cheaper in bulk, even after delivery. Order bulk for beds and lawns, bags only for small patches, and have the truck spot the pile where a wheelbarrow, not a long carry, finishes the job.
